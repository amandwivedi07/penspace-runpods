# Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman's **Thinking, Fast and Slow** distills 40+ years of research into
how the mind actually makes decisions [1]. Published in 2011, it draws on work he
did with Amos Tversky from 1969-1979.

Kahneman describes two systems. *System 1* is fast, automatic and emotional — it
runs constantly and requires no effort. *System 2* is slow, deliberate and
logical; it handles complex computation but is lazy and expensive to engage.

- Roughly 98% of our daily judgments come from System 1.
- System 2 believes itself to be in charge, but usually just ratifies System 1.

The book catalogues systematic biases. Anchoring: when asked whether Gandhi died
before or after age 114, people estimate a far higher age than when the anchor is
35. Availability: we judge frequency by how easily examples come to mind, which
is why people overestimate deaths from plane crashes.

> Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking
> about it.

Prospect theory, which won Kahneman the Nobel Prize in 2002, showed that losses
hurt roughly 2x as much as equivalent gains feel good. A coin flip to win $150 or
lose $100 is rejected by most people, despite the positive expected value.

Kahneman closes on the distinction between the experiencing self and the
remembering self. Ch. 35 argues that we do not choose between experiences; we
choose between memories of experiences, and those memories are dominated by peaks
and endings rather than duration.
